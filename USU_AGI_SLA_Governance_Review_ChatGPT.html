Independent document review · Board / executive working paper

From responsibility map
to executable agreement

A practical review of the October 14, 2026 Draft 2.0 Service Level Agreement between Aspen Group, Inc. and United States University. The focus is whether the agreement makes decision rights, service expectations, evidence, escalation, and institutional safeguards clear enough for both organizations to act consistently.

Source: 19-page SLA Draft 2.0Review basis: supplied document onlyFor discussion, not legal advice

Executive assessment

The draft has a sound governance direction and a useful service-matrix structure. Its main weakness is that several important commitments remain principles or future work rather than fully specified operating obligations.

Good foundationRetained USU authority is stated repeatedly and service areas are organized.
Execution gapMetrics, owners, targets, service levels, and evidence are often deferred until after approval.
Control gapSeveral contractual protections and decision protocols are absent or not sufficiently explicit.
Primary recommendation: Do not treat the matrices alone as a complete SLA. Add a short set of binding schedules that specify service definitions, measurable standards, approval thresholds, data/security controls, financial arrangements, and exit/continuity obligations. Complete the critical schedules before the effective date, not as an indefinite post-signature exercise.

Bottom line: The document is strongest as a governance framework. It needs additional operational and contractual machinery to function reliably as an agreement between a university and its parent-company service provider.

1. What the document is trying to accomplish

The SLA appears intended to do five things:

  1. Describe the shared-services model. AGI supplies centralized corporate and operational services that support USU.
  2. Preserve institutional authority. USU, through its Board, President, faculty governance, and designated officials, retains final authority for academic and institutional matters.
  3. Make the division of work visible. Service matrices identify AGI responsibilities, USU responsibilities, shared coordination, and evidence.
  4. Create oversight and correction mechanisms. The Audit Committee, service owners, performance reviews, and escalation process are intended to identify and resolve deficiencies.
  5. Support accreditation and institutional accountability. The draft references WSCUC policy and emphasizes that USU remains responsible for activities conducted in its name.
Interpretive point: The document is not simply an outsourcing inventory. It is also a boundary-setting instrument: AGI may operate services, but operational support must not quietly become institutional decision-making.

What success should look like: A USU leader can identify the accountable AGI owner, the USU decision-maker, the required approval, the service standard, the evidence to inspect, and the route to intervene when performance or institutional risk is unacceptable.

2. What is working, and what needs strengthening

Strengths to preserve

  • Clear statement that corporate ownership does not transfer reserved academic authority (pp. 5–6).
  • Board and President roles are acknowledged, with Audit Committee review (pp. 5–6).
  • Service matrices separate AGI, USU, and shared coordination (pp. 7–16).
  • Performance standards require metric definitions, targets, frequency, source, owner, and exception criteria (p. 17).
  • Corrective action includes root cause, approved plans, verification, and escalation (p. 18).
  • Employer register and legal-employer confirmation are recognized as pre-execution items (p. 12).

Gaps to close

  • Many metrics have no actual target, service window, severity level, or remedy.
  • Decision rights are stated broadly, but thresholds and approval workflows are not consistently defined.
  • Shared coordination can be mistaken for joint authority unless the boundary is made operational.
  • Data, cybersecurity, privacy, vendor, incident, and transition provisions need more detailed controls.
  • Fees, cost allocation mechanics, term, termination rights, dispute resolution, liability, and indemnity are not developed in the supplied draft.
  • There is no complete schedule of service owners, named delegates, contact routes, or reporting templates.

These are document-review observations, not conclusions that a topic is absent from every related contract or University policy. Confirm whether separate agreements or policies already cover any apparent gap, and cross-reference them explicitly.

4. Suggested order of work

Resolve the items in this sequence so the parties do not polish reporting details while foundational authority and contract mechanics remain unsettled.

SequenceWorkstreamExpected outputSuggested lead
1Authority and governanceDecision-rights schedule, approval matrix, reserved-matters list, named delegates.USU President / BOT counsel or governance lead
2Service definition and standardsCompleted service catalogue, service owners, targets, severity definitions, reporting templates.USU and AGI service owners
3Risk and compliance controlsData/security schedule, incident protocol, audit access, regulatory notification and records controls.USU compliance / security / legal with AGI counterparts
4Commercial and legal termsFees and allocations, term, termination, liability, indemnity, insurance, dispute and change mechanisms.Authorized USU and AGI legal / finance leads
5Readiness and approvalEvidence that all preconditions are met; Board and authorized-party approvals; signed schedules.USU President / BOT as applicable and AGI authorized officer
Execution gate: The current draft says targets, employer assignments, and financial delegation parameters must be confirmed before execution (p. 18). Make this a formal checklist with a named verifier. Do not allow the effective date to arrive with critical schedules marked “to be determined.”
