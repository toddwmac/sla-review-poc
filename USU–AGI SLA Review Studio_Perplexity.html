What this document is trying to do

The SLA defines the shared-services relationship between the university and its owner: AGI (a Phoenix-based education technology holding company) delivers marketing, finance, IT, HR, business office, academic support, and compliance services, while USU — through its Board of Trustees, President, and faculty governance — retains final authority over everything done in the university’s name. It is written to satisfy WSCUC accreditation expectations for documented oversight of outsourced functions.

The draft’s architecture is sound: eight service matrices in a consistent five-column format (deliverable, AGI responsibility, USU responsibility, shared coordination, measure/evidence), a performance-standards section, a corrective-action ladder, and an explicit statement that shared coordination never transfers approval authority to AGI. The suggestions below are aimed at making the agreement easier to act on: most gaps are matters of specificity — undefined terms, pending targets, missing calendars and rosters — rather than structural defects.

What already works well

Retained authority, stated plainly

The Definitions section enumerates exactly what USU keeps — curriculum, admissions, faculty, grading, degrees, complaints, accreditation — and repeats that ownership does not transfer authority. (p. 5)

Consistent five-column matrices

Every service area uses the same deliverable / AGI / USU / shared / evidence structure, which replaced overlapping tables and makes responsibilities directly comparable. (pp. 7–16)

Evidence-first review

Each matrix row names the artifact that proves performance, and the Audit Committee reviews results biannually with met / needs-improvement / did-not-meet ratings. (pp. 6, 17)

Immediate-risk carve-out

Risks to students, compliance, or accreditation must be reported and protected without waiting for the next scheduled review. (p. 18)

Data portability & destruction

The MIS matrix guarantees export capability, usable return formats, and verified destruction of remaining copies. (p. 9)

Employer-of-record discipline

HR requires a verified employer register and states that administrative services do not establish the legal employer. (p. 12)
