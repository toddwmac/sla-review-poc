Executive assessment

Decision summary

Endorse the framework in principle only

Do not authorize signature or effectiveness on the basis of this assessment. The reviews broadly find that Draft 2.0 is a useful governance framework, but it needs more detail to function as an operating agreement.

Where the reviews align

Clarify who decides, define measurable service standards, set escalation deadlines, make transition obligations testable, and verify the employer and system registers.

Where decisions remain

Decide when incident notice is triggered, what must be complete before signing, how materiality is defined, and how Board approval is recorded.

Broad alignment

Strong agreement

The reviews identify the same needs, but do not necessarily agree on wording, thresholds, or deadlines.

Decision rights

Specify who at USU may approve or validate decisions, what AGI may recommend or carry out, and where written authorization is required.

Measurable service standards

Set targets, calculation rules, accountable owners, evidence sources, reporting frequency, and exceptions. Evidence that work occurred is not a performance standard.

Escalation and corrective action

Specify severity levels, notice deadlines, accountable contacts, and the response required when a standard is missed.

Data return and service transition

Specify export formats, timelines, transition support, and secure disposition. Test whether returned data are complete and usable.

Employer-of-record register

Verify the legal employer for covered positions and distinguish AGI’s administrative support from authority over University personnel decisions.

System inventory

Maintain a controlled register of system names, owners, and uses. Resolve undefined names such as “CNS.”

Support from several reviews

Moderate agreement

Several reviews address these areas, but the proposed controls vary in scope and detail.

Commercial and legal terms

ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity call for clearer term, termination, and shared-cost provisions. Perplexity favors a term section inside the SLA. Claude accepts an SLA section or a companion agreement; ChatGPT recommends identifying any governing agreement and specifying which document prevails if they conflict.

References: ChatGPT R06, R07, R19 · Claude S03 · Perplexity F-01, F-11

Board oversight and independence

Several reviews support a defined Audit Committee calendar, standard evidence packages, and timely reporting to trustees. “Twice a year,” with scheduled dates and submission deadlines, is clearer than “biannually.”

Regulatory accountability

Identify who prepares each filing, which USU official validates or approves it, when it is due, and where the approval record is kept.

Narrower proposals worth considering

ChatGPT and Claude propose AI or algorithmic safeguards. Conflict-of-interest rules, access to independent counsel, and whistleblower routing appear in fewer reviews. ChatGPT alone proposes optional service credits or fee adjustments; the other reviews’ silence is not endorsement or rejection.

Divergence and implementation choices

Disagreements & open choices

The notice trigger is the clearest direct conflict. The other differences concern sequencing, drafting, or approval mechanics.

Direct conflict

When does the security notice clock start?

Perplexity proposes initial notice within 24 hours of a confirmed incident. ChatGPT says not to wait for confirmation. Copilot proposes a 24-hour window without specifying when the clock starts.

Resolve: Define which suspected incidents qualify for notice, what starts the clock, and how deadlines vary by severity.

ChatGPT R04 · Perplexity F-12 · Copilot escalation table

Sequencing choice

What is required before signature versus effectiveness?

Perplexity would block signature until the metrics exhibit is approved. ChatGPT calls for measurable service levels before signing and separately proposes an effectiveness gate for mandatory schedules. Copilot requests a metrics appendix and employer register but does not make either a pre-signature condition.

Resolve: List the requirements for approval in principle, signature, and effectiveness separately.

ChatGPT R02, R03 · Perplexity F-02, F-04 · Copilot #2, #6

Drafting choice

Should materiality be uniform or tailored?

Perplexity proposes one agreement-wide definition. Other reviews emphasize thresholds or approval rules suited to specific functions such as spending, technology changes, and outsourcing.

Resolve: Consider a common risk baseline with additional thresholds where a function needs a more precise approval trigger.

Perplexity F-03 · Claude S09 · Copilot #3

Governance choice

How should the Board evidence approval?

Perplexity proposes a Board Chair signature or secretary attestation. ChatGPT leaves open whether a Board resolution and delegated execution suffice.

Resolve: Check USU’s governing documents and record who approves the agreement, who attests to that approval, and who may sign.

Perplexity F-05 · ChatGPT R23 · Claude S17

Recommended action

Before signing

  1. Confirm authority. Settle USU’s reserved decisions, delegations, materiality thresholds, and required Board action.
  2. Approve the operating schedules. Finalize service metrics, the incident protocol, and the service-owner roster. Verify employer assignments and the critical system inventory.
  3. Confirm the commercial and exit terms. Document the cost-allocation basis, termination rights, and transition obligations in the SLA or precisely identify the agreement that governs them.
  4. Record the approval requirements. For each required item, identify the owner, approver, and evidence of completion. Separate signing requirements from any remaining conditions to effectiveness.
