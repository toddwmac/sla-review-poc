## 1. Executive Summary

The three layers of review tell one coherent story:

> **The MOS review was a governance-boundary review — *who controls what*. Draft 2.0 incorporated it thoroughly, in many places nearly verbatim. The four AI reviews, conducted six weeks later and without knowledge of the MOS cycle, independently confirm that the governance layer landed: their "strengths to preserve" lists describe exactly the provisions MOS required. But the AI reviews probe a different question — *is the agreement executable?* — and there they find the next generation of gaps: numeric targets still "pending approval," escalation and incident clocks undefined, exit obligations untestable, and commercial/legal terms absent. These are mostly **beyond the scope MOS was asked to review**, with five specific threads (below) where a MOS concern remains genuinely open in Draft 2.0.**

**Headline numbers:**

| MOS disposition in Draft 2.0 | Comments | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Closed** | MOS1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 12, 13, 14, 18 (10 items) | Required language present in v2; no AI review disputes closure |
| **Closed with new residuals** | MOS2, 7, 15 (3 items) | MOS ask satisfied; AI reviews flag adjacent polish/detail issues |
| **Structurally addressed, execution deferred** | MOS9, 10, 11, 16 (4 items) | The *framework* MOS required now exists, but the operative deliverable (values, registers, exhibits) is still "pending" — this is precisely where the four AI reviews concentrate |
| **Partially addressed** | MOS19 (1 item) | Terminology largely standardized; "CNS" and related residuals remain |
| **Not itemized in the summary** | MOS17 (1 item) | The board summary references "all 19 comments" but does not summarize MOS17; disposition could not be assessed from the file provided |

**Validation point worth emphasizing to the Board:** the AI reviewers never saw the MOS comments — they reviewed Draft 2.0 cold. That an independent four-model panel listed, as strengths, the very provisions MOS forced into the document (reserved-authority statement, consolidated matrices, the performance-standards framework, the employer-register gate) is genuine, unbiased evidence that **the MOS revision cycle worked**. The AI findings are not a contradiction of that; they are the *next* layer of maturity — from governance framework to executable contract.

## 4. Cross-Layer Synthesis

### 4.1 The five live MOS threads in Draft 2.0

These are the places where a concern raised *before* Draft 2.0 remains genuinely unresolved *in* Draft 2.0 (as opposed to new ground opened by the AI reviews):

| # | Thread | MOS origin | Where it stands in v2 | Who re-flagged it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Numeric performance targets** | MOS16 | Framework added; targets "pending approval" | All four reviews — top consensus finding |
| 2 | **Employer-of-record register** | MOS11 | Register required; verification deferred to pre-execution | R13, #6, F-04 |
| 3 | **Shared-cost methodology & financial thresholds** | MOS10 | Authority stated; methodology/thresholds not attached or quantified | R06, S09, #3, F-03, F-11 |
| 4 | **"CNS" / system inventory** | MOS19 | Acknowledged in Background; deferred to a nonexistent inventory | All four reviews |
| 5 | **Data exit & incident mechanics** | MOS9 | Categories present; no timelines, formats, or clocks | R04/R05/R07, S08/S10, #4/#7, F-12/F-14 |

**Pattern:** in every case Draft 2.0 created the *control* but deferred the *measurement*. The AI reviews uniformly treat the measurement as the condition of enforceability — which is exactly why the fused recommendation is "endorse in principle; complete the schedules before signing/effectiveness."

### 4.2 What the AI reviews found that MOS never raised (new ground)

The MOS review was scoped to governance boundaries. The following — all prominent in the AI consensus — have no MOS antecedent:

- **Commercial and legal architecture:** term, renewal, termination, liability, indemnity, insurance, dispute resolution, governing law (R07, R19, S03, F-01 — 3-of-4 support; the majority treats missing term/exit as *structural*, against Copilot's "operational precision" characterization)
- **Escalation clocks and the incident-notice trigger conflict** ("confirmed" vs. "do not wait for confirmation" — F-12 vs. R04)
- **Board approval mechanics for the SLA itself** (signature blocks currently AGI CEO + USU President only: R23, S17, F-05/F-19)
- **AI/algorithmic governance** (the CRM next-best-action algorithm: R24, S12)
- **Conflict-of-interest and recusal rules** (S05), **independent counsel/privilege/whistleblower routing** (F-13 — noting v2 assigns whistleblower-hotline *administration* to AGI under Legal Support, which sharpens Perplexity's independence concern)
- **Service credits / financial remedies** (R17, uncontested), **RTO/RPO continuity objectives** (R21), **non-operative labeling of the executive letters' promotional claims** (R22, S16)

### 4.3 Where the layers agree

All three layers converge on the same theory of the document: **USU's reserved authority is real and must be operational.** MOS supplied the control language; Draft 2.0 placed it; the AI reviews ask that it be made measurable, clocked, and exit-able. There is no layer-to-layer contradiction on governance substance — the only direct contradiction *within* the corpus (Copilot's "operational precision" vs. the majority's "structural omissions") concerns severity of the post-MOS gaps, not the MOS fixes themselves.

### 4.4 Sequencing note for the Board

Draft 2.0's own Approvals page — "Approval dates, service standards, employer assignments, and financial delegation parameters must be confirmed before execution" — is an internal pre-execution gate that pre-figures the AI reviews' worklist. The open question the fusion panel preserved is whether that gate binds **signature** (Perplexity) or only **effectiveness** (ChatGPT's reading), and whether the Board signs, attests, or delegates. Those are decisions, not drafting gaps, and they belong to the Board.
